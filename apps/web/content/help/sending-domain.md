Before sending any outreach, set up the domain your mailboxes use. It takes about 30 minutes, and it's the single biggest factor in whether your emails reach the inbox or the spam folder.

## Use a separate domain

Send outreach from a **different domain** than your main business email. For example, if your company uses `yourcompany.com`, register something like `getyourcompany.com` or `yourcompany.co` for outreach.

Why: email providers track a reputation for every sending domain. If outreach gets marked as spam, that reputation drops, and it drops for _everything_ sent from that domain, including your invoices, password resets and normal business email. A separate domain keeps that risk contained.

> [!TIP]
> Make the outreach domain redirect to your main website, so anyone who checks it lands somewhere real.

## The three DNS records

Email providers check three records in your domain's DNS settings (managed wherever you bought the domain, or wherever your DNS is hosted, e.g. Cloudflare):

| Record    | What it proves                                                       |                                              |
| --------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| **SPF**   | Which servers may send email for your domain                         | Required                                     |
| **DKIM**  | Each email is really from you and wasn't changed                     | Required                                     |
| **DMARC** | What to do with email that fails SPF/DKIM, and where to send reports | Required by Gmail and Yahoo for bulk senders |

Without all three, many of your emails will go to spam or be rejected.

### SPF

Add a **TXT** record on the domain itself (`@`). For Google Workspace:

```
v=spf1 include:_spf.google.com ~all
```

For Microsoft 365, use `include:spf.protection.outlook.com` instead. A domain must have **only one** SPF record; if one exists, add the `include:` to it.

### DKIM

DKIM keys are generated by your email provider:

- **Google Workspace:** Admin console → Apps → Google Workspace → Gmail → **Authenticate email** → Generate new record. Add the TXT record it shows (usually at `google._domainkey`), wait for DNS to update, then click **Start authentication**.
- **Microsoft 365:** Microsoft Defender portal → Email authentication settings → DKIM. Add the two CNAME records shown, then enable signing.

### DMARC

Add a **TXT** record at `_dmarc`:

```
v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:dmarc-reports@yourdomain.com
```

`p=none` means "monitor only", which is the right start. After a few weeks of clean reports you can tighten it to `p=quarantine`.

## Check your setup

After DNS changes have spread (usually minutes, sometimes a few hours):

1. Send an email from the new mailbox to a personal Gmail address.
2. In Gmail, open it, click the ⋮ menu → **Show original**.
3. Check that SPF, DKIM and DMARC all say **PASS**.

Free online checkers (search for "SPF DKIM DMARC checker") will also report problems.

## Let the domain age

A domain registered yesterday and sending hundreds of emails today looks suspicious. Before starting campaigns:

- use the mailboxes normally for one to two weeks (send and receive real emails),
- then start at a low daily limit and [ramp up slowly](/help/deliverability#ramp-up-new-mailboxes-slowly).
